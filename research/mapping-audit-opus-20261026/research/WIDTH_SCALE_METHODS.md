# Local support, pose consistency, clearance and scale: released methods (research only)

Date: 2026-10-26. Scope: source/literature research only. Nothing was built, installed or run, and no project file was changed.

**How things were checked.** For each repository I ran `git ls-remote` and made a shallow, blob-less clone in the scratchpad (no checkout, no build). From that I recorded the HEAD commit, the newest tag and its commit date, the LICENSE text at HEAD, and the key source files. Papers were read from publisher or arXiv pages and from PDFs (via pdftotext) where they were reachable. Some entries are marked **unverified**: I did not read those claims in the primary source.

**Context found in the repository (read-only).** `research/mapping-audit-opus-20261026/pose-consistency/README.md` already attributes Koivu's floor offsets to pose error that occurs in runs of consecutive frames (frames 60–69 at +5 cm and 116–120 at −3 cm). Its evidence is that wall/furniture-only ICP removes most of the floor offset, and that single-frame ICP is unsafe. `research/koivu-reference-comparison/` already documents the MuSHRoom FARO files `gt_mesh.ply` and `icp_iphone.json`. The recommendations below build on both.

Two literature points bear on that diagnosis:
- Zea & Hanebeck (2022) measured the Apple depth bias as **always negative**: points come out nearer than they really are. With the camera looking down at the floor, that can only *raise* the apparent floor. A bias therefore cannot produce both +5 cm and −3 cm runs, which supports the pose-error explanation.
- The same paper found that Apple depth is a smooth surface that "shifts and deforms between frames", with strong spatial correlation. Some per-frame offset can therefore come from depth even without pose error. A cheap test: regress each frame's floor offset on its median floor range. Range-proportional offsets would point to depth; offsets grouped in time would point to pose.

### Suggested order (adapter work, not done)
1. **Evaluation first, using reference data only.** Render `gt_mesh.ply` into every Koivu frame (Open3D `RaycastingScene`, with the pose transformed by `icp_iphone.json['gt_transformation']`). For each frame, fit residual = a + b·range + a small rigid twist. A large twist means pose error; a large b means depth bias; a residual that follows FARO floor shape means real floor variation. Do not tune on this; it is the scorer.
2. **Phone-only joint refinement.** Build an Open3D pose graph from the recorded relative poses as odometry edges, with an information matrix from a stated drift prior. Add ICP loop edges restricted to non-floor geometry (as in the audit), with information from `GetInformationMatrixFromPointClouds`. Run global LM with line-process pruning (`edge_prune_threshold`). Pass only if held-out frames improve against FARO **and control frames do not get worse**. Cross-check with RTAB-Map reprocessing.
3. **Local support.** Port the elevation_mapping per-cell height/variance fusion onto `grid_map_core`. Seed the sensor variance with the iPad noise model below, marked as a different device. Inflate the variance with each frame's pose covariance. Cells that are unseen, in conflict, or carry variance above a threshold stay `unresolved`.
4. **Clearance.** Use dynamicEDT3D (3-D, both unknown policies) and/or a 2-D EDT plus medial axis on the support-relative obstacle mask. Report passage width as [pessimistic, optimistic]. Speak or claim a width only when both bounds clear the threshold, and only with fresh evidence, per the spatial safety rule.
5. **Scale.** Fit Sim(3) between the phone cloud and FARO and report s − 1. Separately, check one or two tape-measured spans.

**What this means for Koivu:**
- Depth random noise is millimetre-level.
- Depth bias (1–2 % of range, toward the camera) and per-frame correlated deformation are centimetre-level, the same order as the observed offsets. The bias can only raise the floor.
- Pose error of 0.2–1 % of path, or worse without loop closure (Díaz-Vilariño's 30 cm wall doubling), is enough for 3–6 cm vertical and 10–20 cm horizontal misplacement.
- Small objects below ~11 depth pixels and low steps can be flattened into the floor, so "no detection" must stay unresolved, never free.

## 4. Not suitable now, and why

- **nvblox, elevation_mapping_cupy**: need a CUDA GPU (we have none). elevation_mapping_cupy also needs ROS2. Its per-scan height drift compensation is worth copying as a *design idea* only.
- **FIESTA, voxblox, Voxfield, Kimera-Semantics, mav_voxblox_planning**: ROS1/catkin-bound and unmaintained (last commits 2019–2023). They offer no uncertainty beyond TSDF weights. dynamicEDT3D plus a 2-D medial axis covers the clearance need inside the octomap stack we already use.
- **hloc**: needs PyTorch and learned-model weight downloads (prohibited without a go-ahead). Matcher weights carry separate licences, some non-commercial.
- **ETH3D dataset-pipeline**: the closest concept (refine poses and intrinsics against a laser scan), but it has heavy Qt/PCL/OpenGV/OpenGL dependencies, a DSLR-oriented workflow, and no release tags.
- **Patchwork++**: ROS-free and maintained, but tuned for vehicle LiDAR (12.5 cm ground thickness, 2.7 m minimum range). Used as shipped, it would merge steps and small objects into the ground, and it gives no variance.
- **Log-GPIS / GP distance fields**: research code. A follow-up paper reports that its uncertainty is unreliable.
- **COLMAP as primary pose truth for Koivu**: the dataset authors report that COLMAP failed on the Koivu iPhone sequence. Use it only as a secondary photometric check.
- **RoomPlan dimensions as evidence**: no published dimensional accuracy. Detection P/R is not a measurement, and the RoomPlan-based RASSAR does not quantify it.
- **NavAssist** (GitHub, blind-navigation iPhone app): **unverified** (licence and commit not checked). Its README describes a fixed 60 cm corridor, not a measured width.

No 2023–2026 released and reproducible method was found for measuring passage or door width from phone LiDAR with uncertainty, for either blind or wheelchair navigation. RASSAR (2024, MIT) relies on RoomPlan dimensions. The point-cloud wayfinding papers found use fixed erosion widths (e.g. 0.8 m pedestrian / 1 m wheelchair) or monocular depth. This is a **recorded gap**: the width-interval method in rank 2 is our own composition of released primitives (EDT, medial axis, unknown policy), and must be validated on Koivu against FARO and on tape-measured passages. It must not be described as an established method.
