# Assistant evidence and speech policy

Priority is deterministic: urgent sensor hazards and tracking/calibration problems preempt language generation; route cues use validated app state; user questions come next; optional ambient descriptions are last. Missing evidence must remain missing. A camera classification is not an object coordinate or a safe route.

VoiceOver's general image-description feature is not an input to this mobility pipeline. The app selects facts and generates short text first. When VoiceOver is running, UIKit accessibility announcements deliver that text; otherwise AVSpeechSynthesizer does. Automatic ambient descriptions pause under VoiceOver so interface exploration remains usable. Urgent speech reserves a five-second interruption window against ordinary app speech; stopping descriptions preserves that window. Physical testing must verify announcement interruption, headset behavior, language, timing and user control. iOS manages VoiceOver scheduling, so software guards are not a guarantee of audible delivery.

SceneContext filters observations by phone monotonic time (at most three seconds), confidence and requested detail before the language stage. Automatic descriptions require stationary behavior, non-minimal detail and monitoring in the observing state. The app deduplicates and spaces ambient speech. Delivery rejects stale camera frames, answers older than twelve seconds, translations over half a metre, or horizontal camera turns over thirty degrees. Vertical view changes need additional validation; these gates do not establish full visual equivalence.

Wearable range facts have a stricter 800 ms freshness limit, require calibrated fault-free packets and omit invalid distances. At most two facts enter the description budget, leaving room for camera evidence. Belt facts use body-relative sectors; wrist facts explicitly refer to its pointing direction. A range does not identify an object or supply a world-map coordinate. Before speaking a pending answer, the app rejects changed/disconnected range evidence. A confidence value on a validated packet is not a measured physical accuracy probability.

Offline Vision supplies recognized text, detected people counts and scene categories. If available, Apple's on-device language model selects existing evidence IDs; spoken descriptions are rendered from evidence templates. This constrains invented observations. Without that model the same deterministic templates work. This is not a complete offline visual question-answering model.

An additional experimental RGB stair check now runs locally, at most once per second, independently of descriptions and depth processing. It checks the runtime `VNClassifyImageRequest.supportedIdentifiers()` list; if no exact supported stair label is available, it reports that limitation instead of pretending stair recognition works. A matching score of at least 0.75 can add a fixed Arabic/English "possible stairs" warning, with no distance, direction of ascent/descent, or body-relative haptic bearing. That threshold is an unvalidated prototype choice, not a reliability percentage. Freshness (1.5 seconds), current tracked pose (0.5 seconds), displacement (0.3 m), horizontal turn and pitch change (15 degrees) limit delivery. A picture of stairs on a poster can still cause a false positive; actual false-alarm/miss rates are unknown. Measured drop/overhead warnings outrank it; measured obstacles take precedence at equal priority. No image output grants clearance. The classifier has not run here because Apple Vision is unavailable on Windows.

Phone depth sampling now keeps every second row/column rather than every fourth, while retaining the highest confidence filter. Recent measured ground is retained for at most 0.25 seconds and discarded after excessive movement/turn or measurement interruption; it changes only a recheck explanation, never fills missing ground or clears a warning. After uncertainty, guidance requires one continuous second in the observing state to recover. New hazards bypass this recovery hold. Unchanged uncertainty repeats after 15 seconds, and the Guide screen has an explicit repeat-status button. The raw detector's missing-ground rules remain unchanged. These are prototype timing choices requiring device and comprehension tests.

Optional online photo descriptions use an authenticated relay and the OpenAI Responses image input. The system prompt distinguishes surroundings, room, people, object, text and visible path features; it uses camera-relative locations and states uncertainty. It does not identify people or infer unseen floor plans. Map geometry and journeys are not uploaded. Provider output is not semantically proven by prompt instructions; evaluate it on representative consented images before relying on its quality.

The photo prompt prioritizes visible possible stairs, drops, open edges and blocking obstacles before ordinary requested details. This is an instruction for the optional spoken answer, not a structured LLM-to-motor or LLM-to-clearance channel. Subsequent contextual-prompt tests used synthetic text only; actual image hazard reasoning remains untested. Image reasoning has documented limitations, including precise spatial localization: [official OpenAI image/vision guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision). Apple's runtime label discovery is documented in [VNClassifyImageRequest](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/vision/vnclassifyimagerequest).

2026-10-08 relay update: optional situation context includes room hierarchy, current/last-seen entities, distinct camera/body pose, goal/preferences/history, bounded conversation and arrival. The relay preserves detailed mode during movement, follows the current conversational goal and enforces a per-request word allowance. Later native source now adds GRDB memory, independent rolling word/interval controls, automatic VoiceOver and moving-detail changes, explicit interests and an optional Apple image adapter. These native changes await Apple/device verification. No valid pose or navigation command is inferred from a description. See [native handoff](NATIVE_MEMORY_HANDOFF.md) and [latest continuation](COMMUNITY_IMPLEMENTATION_HANDOFF.md).

Word/sentence targets: minimal or moving, 25 words/one sentence; balanced while stationary, 55/two; detailed while stationary, 110/four. These are prompt targets, not a verified sentence-count constraint. Hazard interruption is controlled by the app, not the LLM. Speech should be simple and respectful for both adults and children; users and their mobility specialist choose detail and language.

No fine-tuning job or image training dataset exists. Start with evaluation: correct/incorrect landmarks, unreadable signs, duplicate restroom names, occluded objects, missing destinations, stale answers, moving crowds, apparent stairs with unknown direction, and text that tries to instruct the model. Score factual support, explicit uncertainty, verbosity, latency and whether hazards interrupt. Use consented staged scenes without unnecessary children's identifiable data. Fine-tuning would only follow demonstrated shortcomings and a reviewed dataset, not replace geometry or freshness checks.

References: [RNIB guiding advice](https://www.rnib.org.uk/living-with-sight-loss/supporting-others/guiding-a-blind-or-partially-sighted-person/) supports asking preferences and describing the location/direction of hazards; [Perkins orientation concepts](https://www.perkins.org/resource/concept-development-putting-orientation-back-om/) informs landmark-based explanations. [Apple on-device speech capability](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/speech/sfspeechrecognizer/supportsondevicerecognition) is checked before recording with on-device recognition required. [OpenAI image inputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/images-vision) describe the relay's image payload; [supervised fine-tuning guidance](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/supervised-fine-tuning) recommends establishing evaluations first.
