# Elevation, cliff and obstacle detection research

Reviewed 2026-10-02. This is a design and verification record for a wearable prototype, not evidence of reliable child mobility. The belt is a core part of the intended system. First valid danger evidence must warn locally; neither another sensor nor the LLM may veto or delay that warning. A missing return is unknown ground, not a clear path.

These repositories' tests are useful references. We have not run their ROS/PX4 suites in this workspace. Our own Swift/C++ fault-injection tests run here; the separately recorded Recast tests concern map navigation geometry, not cliff sensing. Preserve per-file licenses and dependencies before importing code; repository popularity is not validation for children.

Source inspection: Nav2's [Polygon trigger implementation](https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/main/nav2_collision_monitor/src/polygon.cpp) keeps separate trigger and release counters. That supports the distinction used here: immediate onset after valid evidence, filtered release. We did not copy its implementation or adopt its multi-source point-count threshold as a requirement for sensor agreement.

## Morning, direct sunlight and night

The meaningful variables are illumination at the sensor, return quality, surface, distance, viewing angle and motion—not the clock time. Low morning/evening sun can point directly into the receiver. Test both facing the sun and with the sun behind the rig.

The current downward part is **TFmini-S**, not TFmini Plus. Its manufacturer lists a 2-degree field of view, 70 klux ambient-light resistance, and nominal accuracy of +/-6 cm at 0.1–6 m. The accuracy footnote uses a 90% reflective white target indoors at 25 C and says other conditions can increase error. Therefore those figures cannot establish detection of a small dark stair edge outdoors. Source: [TFmini-S datasheet](https://en.benewake.com/uploadfiles/2024/04/20240426140025367.pdf).

The VL53L5CX uses its own infrared emitter, so ranging does not require visible room light. However, ambient infrared and target reflectance affect usable range. One documented 8x8/15 Hz/5 ms autonomous-mode example changes a white target's inner-zone typical range from 1.9 m in darkness to 0.9 m at 5 klux. This is **not our ring's 4x4/5 Hz configuration**; it illustrates why the headline “4 metres” is not a guaranteed outdoor detection distance. Source: [ST datasheet, tables and test conditions](https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/vl53l5cx.pdf).

RGB identification/OCR and visual localization need separate low-light testing even when an active range sensor still returns distances. Ultrasound offers a different physical measurement channel, but its effective beam depends on target size and distance; it cannot simply replace a narrow ground ray. Test soft, sloped and reflective surfaces. Source: [MaxBotix beam-pattern guidance](https://maxbotix.com/pages/reading-maxsonar-beam-patterns). The existing sonar faces forward and is **not a second ground modality**. No radar or thermal cliff detector is implemented or selected.

Engineering conclusion: complementary sensors can improve coverage, but two infrared devices can fail together in the same lighting/surface conditions. An ordinary RGB image or an LLM saying “stairs” must never clear invalid ground sensing. Darkness, glare or weak returns must reduce permitted guidance, not lower validity thresholds to keep the app talking.

## Changes made in this project

- Phone: the first frame containing credible spatial drop/obstacle/overhead evidence triggers that specific warning. The previous three-frame onset wait is removed. A single erroneous depth pixel still does not establish a surface. Sparse, weak or missing ground returns cause an immediate uncertainty warning.
- Phone: require repeated support samples in both central columns across the forward floor corridor. Near and far floor patches no longer conceal an unseen middle gap. The approximate 4 cm support band and 9 cm hazard threshold are prototype assumptions, not measured sensor accuracy or universal safe step heights.
- Belt: either calibrated downward channel can trigger on its first fresh increased-range sample. The other channel need not agree or update simultaneously. A simultaneous fault on the other channel cannot mask that danger. Three fresh normal readings are required to release that channel's warning; duplicate reads cannot count as recovery.
- Belt: a sufficiently shorter ground range now raises an obstacle warning too. Its raised-ground qualifier prevents unrelated ring ranges from supplying a false direction/distance. Longer and shorter readings can coexist; both remain danger and drop has priority. The existing slant-range threshold still needs physical redesign/validation.
- Belt: loss of ground sensing latches a fault until explicit recalibration and preserves an already detected drop. Local warning pulses outrank phone navigation commands even between pulses. The wrist also uses immediate local warning transitions instead of waiting for its distance-pulse interval.
- Phone BLE callback publishes a received belt warning immediately rather than waiting for the watchdog timer. Optional questions/descriptions are cancelled for danger or monitoring loss. The wrist remains a pointing instrument: its local haptic is not falsely described as an obstacle straight ahead of the body.

“Immediate” means no intentional confirmation/LLM delay after valid detection. Acquisition, loop scheduling, bus transactions, Bluetooth and audio/haptic hardware still have latency; those latencies have not been measured. Current phone sampling is limited to 15 Hz and ring ranging is configured at 5 Hz. A software test cannot establish the time from an actual edge entering view to a child feeling/hearing the warning.

## Unresolved elevation design requirements

1. **Rigid belt attitude measurement.** Current belt firmware has no integrated belt IMU. A wrist IMU cannot determine belt pitch. Establish each ground sensor's mounting transform, height and tilt uncertainty before projecting slant ranges into ground height and forward distance. Account for bending, running, loose clothing and mount shifts. Reject unsupported attitudes. A constant slant-range baseline alone is insufficient.
2. **Coverage before the feet reach danger.** Measure the nearest continuously monitored ground corridor across body width and intended turns. Include first stair tread, isolated curb, small step, open edge with no far return, descending ramp, sloped floor, glass and black carpet. Sideways/backwards motion needs explicit coverage or a stop limitation.
3. **Warning distance budget.** Proposed engineering budget: `required look-ahead = upper-bound speed × (measurement age + processing + alert delivery + measured response time) + measured stopping distance after response + position/edge uncertainty + margin`. Do not double-count response travel inside stopping distance. No default child's reaction time is assumed. If required terms or adequate observed range are unavailable, the device cannot claim adequate warning distance.
4. **Ground threshold review.** The belt still uses a **250 mm change in slant range**, not a 250 mm stair height. This is not adequate evidence of small-step detection. Changing it to an arbitrary smaller number before measuring noise/tilt does not solve that problem. The phone's lower geometry threshold does not compensate for an uncovered belt blind spot.
5. **Local operation and recovery.** Measure warnings with phone disconnected, LLM busy, radio congestion, I2C fault, sensor saturation, reboot, low voltage, covered sensor and failed haptic. Check that restarting or recalibrating never silently declares an unknown route usable. Motor drive calibration and delivery feedback remain unfinished.

## Physical test matrix and evidence

Use a fixed rig/adult bench setup and guarded fixtures first, not children approaching exposed drops. Repeat the same measured geometry under darkness, indoor lighting, shaded outdoor light, low-angle sunlight, direct midday sun and fast transitions between them. Record lux as context, but retain sensor signal/status because visible lux alone does not characterize infrared interference.

For each condition vary floor reflectance/material, edge height, distance, approach angle, speed, belt height/pitch, partial occlusion and sensor dropout. Record device/firmware versions, raw samples and validity, acquisition/alert timestamps, known fixture geometry, earliest warning distance, missed warnings, late warnings, false alarms and recovery. Include unchanged floor trials to quantify false alarms. Keep a held-out set of surfaces and lighting conditions after tuning thresholds. One successful staircase demonstration or zero misses in a small sample is not a reliability guarantee.

The real 3DLite apartment mesh is useful for geometry/import tests only: it contains no raw daylight/night sensing, no ground-truth stair labels and no measured human reaction. It cannot validate this matrix. Adult engineering validation and orientation-and-mobility review remain necessary before deciding on supervised child use.

## Missing depth, prediction and repeated warnings — 2026-10-03

Do not conflate an actual physical gap/height change with a gap in measured data. Missing depth can come from sampling, occlusion, reflectance, a missing reconstruction surface or a real edge. Its apparent pixel size does not establish a safe physical width or depth. The earlier missed 4–12 cm belt cases are actual synthetic height changes, not missing pixels.

Current code uses a continuous ARKit camera session during foreground monitoring and processes scene depth at up to 15 Hz. `DepthSampler` retains the highest confidence tier. Following the density experiment, it now samples every second row/column (one in four depth pixels), up from one in 16; device performance is unmeasured. It uses instantaneous `sceneDepth`, not `smoothedSceneDepth`; Apple describes the latter as measurements averaged across frames for smoother visual effects. Smoothing is not a validated cliff detector. See [Apple depth semantics](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/arkit/arconfiguration/framesemantics-swift.struct/smoothedscenedepth).

Scene identification is a separate on-demand operation on a current RGB frame. Optional automatic local descriptions attempt at most every four seconds, gated on attention and monitoring, with further speech suppression. The cloud receives a JPEG only through the explicit enabled cloud action. Optional native mapping consumes frames at most twice per second into its local database. Normal monitoring does not record a movie. Backgrounding stops phone monitoring; RoomPlan scanning pauses the phone hazard loop. Battery and temperature are unmeasured.

The hazard core still decides clearance from the current frame. A new diagnostic measured-ground history (up to 0.25 seconds) changes recheck wording only and is invalidated by excessive pose change or interruption. A separate continuity filter holds uncertainty through one second of stable observing results, reducing state oscillation; new danger bypasses it. `AlertPolicy` speaks on state changes and repeats unchanged uncertainty after 15 seconds. The repeat-status button provides an explicit user request. No delayed-onset hazard filter or predicted clearance is introduced.

Relevant primary research and implementations:

- [ANYbotics elevation_mapping](https://github.com/ANYbotics/elevation_mapping) fuses measurements and pose covariance, preserving height variance. Use its measured-evidence/uncertainty concepts when designing local ground history. A prior map needs aging and current visibility checks; it cannot clear a new drop.
- [ANYbotics grid_map](https://github.com/ANYbotics/grid_map) includes an OpenCV `InpaintFilter` for filling holes. An interpolated layer must remain distinct from measured ground. Filled cells alone cannot authorize walking.
- [Solving Occlusion in Terrain Mapping](https://github.com/mstoelzle/solving-occlusion) provides the authors' learned terrain-completion implementation. The [paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2109.07150v2) demonstrates planning with predicted terrain and explicitly identifies prediction uncertainty as further work. It is not a validated solution for guiding children. No model was imported or run here.
- [CMU negative-obstacle detection](https://publications.ri.cmu.edu/potential-negative-obstacle-detection-by-occlusion-labeling) interprets missing information and occlusion as possible negative-obstacle evidence instead of assuming that unmeasured ground is flat.

Proposed next design: preserve separate measured, predicted and unknown states; improve ground-region sampling; fuse recent measured points only with bounded age, stable tracking and position uncertainty; keep new raw danger on an immediate independent path; use predictions to choose where to look again and to propose routes needing verification. Match warning urgency to the approaching footpath and measured response-distance budget. A distant unknown region may prompt an earlier rescan, but immediate unknown footing still blocks walking guidance. These changes need held-out drop, narrow-gap, stale-map and sensor-loss tests before production use.

Cause-specific recovery text is now implemented for tracking loss, unavailable depth/frame, unsupported camera orientation/height, and insufficient ground support. Each starts with "Stop" and supplies a stationary recovery instruction. It does not diagnose sunlight or a blocked lens merely from missing measurements. Reminder spacing and recovery holding are implemented as described above; physical wording, timing and comprehension tests remain required. Broader uncertainty-aware terrain fusion and learned completion remain research work; no predicted floor authorizes walking.
